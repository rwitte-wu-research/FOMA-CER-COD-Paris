# Title

Did the Paris Agreement Reprice Corporate Environmental Responsibility in Credit Markets? A Meta-Analysis of the Cost of Debt

# Running title

Paris Agreement and the Cost of Debt

<!-- 36 characters; pre-staged for the M7 title-page assembly (double-blind: title pages ship separately). -->

# Abstract

Whether the Paris Agreement repriced corporate environmental responsibility (CER) in credit markets is a central expectation of sustainable finance that no synthesis has tested. We meta-analyze the CER–cost of debt relationship, pooling 2,713 effect sizes from 115 studies forming 114 independent author clusters, with three-level cluster-robust models and pre-specified equivalence tests. The pooled association is statistically detectable but economically negligible — r = −0.059 (95% CI [−0.086; −0.031]), 0.84 times the conventional small-effect benchmark for partial correlations — and explicitly not a null effect. Centrally, the post-minus-pre Paris contrast is statistically equivalent to zero within the pre-specified smallest-effect-size-of-interest band: no detectable Paris-Agreement moderation. No coded measurement, instrument, industry, regulatory, or country context detectably shifts the association, and publication-selection corrections mark the pooled magnitude as an upper bound. We convert a scattered literature into a calibrated quantity and give the Paris-repricing thesis its first meta-analytic test in credit-market pricing.
